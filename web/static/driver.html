What this screen is

The navigation display mounted in the ambulance cab (or the operator's phone). It runs from the same route object the control centre uses to open the green corridor — the driver and the traffic lights are literally reading one plan.

What the driver sees

• The road ahead, heading-up, with the corridor drawn in green.
• The next signal's state before reaching it — "will be GREEN for you" means the control centre has purposely enabled it for this unit; a corridor open for another ambulance, a disarmed system or lights off are each named instead.
• Speed beside the posted limit sign; above the limit with lights on, the EMERGENCY EXEMPT badge shows — enforcement cameras log the pass and issue no citation.
• Live arrival time on the Kuwait clock.

Why it matters

The driver never wonders whether the next junction knows about them. While priority is armed and the lights are on, a junction on the route is either enabled for this unit or running its normal cycle until the unit is close enough to claim it — and when no priority is being requested at all, the card says so rather than promising a corridor.

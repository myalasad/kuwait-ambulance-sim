Every 30 s a 5-minute forecast is filed for each corridor and later scored against the state that actually occurred. Persistence = "stays as it is now"; climatology = the long-run average. Skill is the Brier skill score versus climatology: 1 = perfect, 0 = no better than guessing the average, negative = worse. If the chains add nothing, this says so.

Each corridor is a 4-state chain (FREE/SLOW/CONGESTED/JAMMED). A DTMC gives the long-run (stationary) congested share; a CTMC generator gives P(state at any horizon) — that forecast feeds the router, which weighs each road by its predicted speed at the moment the ambulance will reach it, not just its speed now. The chains learn from the running simulation and persist across sessions — the model feeds itself constantly. (Learning source here is simulated traffic; the identical estimator runs on real detector data when available.)

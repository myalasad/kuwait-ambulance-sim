Operating Protocol

Live Map Driver Navigation Operations Protocol How it works

This is the complete rulebook the control system follows. Every rule here is implemented in the simulation and every application of a rule is recorded as a structured operation with a case id — visible live on the Operations page and persisted to data/operations.jsonl for after-action review.

1Normal corridor operation

Camera detection → confirmation → green corridor

Each signalized junction has a virtual camera that recognises an ambulance with active emergency lights up to 200 m along its approaches. The first detection confirms the vehicle to the control centre, which knows its planned route (the same Dijkstra route the driver navigates — see Navigation). Signals along the route are then purposely enabled in sequence when the ambulance's ETA drops below 25 s: amber to conflicting traffic (3 s), all-red clearance (2 s), then the junction's own programme phase serving the ambulance's approach is held. The queue in front of the ambulance discharges through the green — cars ahead get priority, exactly as intended. Signals are never switched dark: a dark signal causes collisions.

Case P-nnn opens at enablement, closes at restoration; the map labels the junction "PURPOSELY ENABLED" and then "BACK TO NORMAL".

2Two priority vehicles — ambulance vs ambulance

2a. Clear margin — automatic arbitration

When two corridors request the same junction and one ambulance is clearly nearer (margin > 20 m), the nearest is granted and the second queues behind the decision. The arbitration is logged as a warning-level operation naming both vehicles and both distances.

2b. Tie — unable to decide → refer to operator

If the margin is under 20 m, the controller declares itself unable to decide. The junction is held on its normal programme — the safe state — a D-case opens, and the conflict is pushed to the operator on the Live Map and the Operations page with one-click supervisor grant buttons. If no human decides within 8 s, the default policy (nearest) is applied automatically and logged as a policy decision.

2c. Continuity rule

A junction already serving a corridor keeps serving it until that ambulance passes — switching allegiance mid-approach would trap both streams in the box. The follower is served immediately after.

2d. Same approach — shared corridor

Two ambulances on the same approach share the same green; the second re-arms the junction during its clearing window if needed.

3Errors and fail-safes

3a. Signal command failure

If any command to a junction fails (communication/state error), the junction is immediately reverted to its normal programme — never left frozen in a corridor state — the P-case closes with status error, and an error-severity operation is emitted for the operator.

3b. Simulation/server failure

If the simulation loop dies, the server broadcasts the error to every connected dashboard (no silent freeze), keeps serving, and offers Reset. The run_forever.sh watchdog restarts the whole server if the process itself dies. In a real deployment the analogous fail-safe is the local junction controller falling back to its fixed-time plan when the centre stops responding.

3c. Over-held junction (starvation guard)

A single hold is capped at 90 s. Beyond it, cross traffic is guaranteed a normal cycle (20 s cooldown) unless the ambulance is already at the stop line. Both the release and the re-arm are logged on the case.

3d. Ambulance physically inside the junction

A junction is never restored while the ambulance is inside its box — restoring early would give cross traffic green into a box the ambulance still occupies.

4Why did the ambulance disappear / lose its lights?

Nothing leaves the map silently. Every ambulance has an A-case from dispatch to close, and the close reason is always one of these logged operations:

4a. Arrived (normal)

AMB_n ARRIVED at its destination and was removed from the map (run complete) — with travel time, distance, average speed and the planned ETA for comparison.

4b. Teleported by the congestion resolver

SUMO teleports any vehicle physically stuck for > 180 s. If that happens to an ambulance its position jumps; the operation says so explicitly and flags it as a simulation artefact, not a comms loss.

4c. Lights switched off (operator action)

The operator can switch an ambulance's emergency lights off/on from the Live Map (per-ambulance toggle). Lights off means it stops requesting priority: its junctions restore to normal, its route overlay dims, and the who/when/why is logged on its case. Switching back on resumes corridor requests.

4d. Unexpected removal

If an active ambulance vanishes from the vehicle list without an arrival event, an error operation fires immediately (LEFT THE SIMULATION UNEXPECTEDLY) and the case closes with status error for investigation.

4e. Reset

A dashboard Reset restarts the world; open cases from the previous run remain in data/operations.jsonl with their last state.

5Operator & supervisor powers

  • Grant priority in a referred conflict (2b) — one click, logged by name ("supervisor").
  • Arm/disarm the whole preemption system — disarming releases every junction through the normal amber-down sequence and is logged.
  • Lights on/off per ambulance (4c).
  • Pause / resume / reset the simulation; every state change is broadcast to all connected screens so no operator sees a stale picture.

6Routing (navigation = corridor)

One route object, two consumers

Dispatch computes the route with Dijkstra over the network's edge graph — one-way streets and OSM turn restrictions respected, edges weighted by live travel times so the route avoids congestion at dispatch time. The same route is assigned to the driver (vehicle navigation) and read by the control centre (which junction comes next, at what ETA). That is how a junction knows the ambulance is coming before its camera ever sees it: detection confirms; the route predicts. Full node-by-node analysis is on the Navigation page.

Automatic reroute to the nearest hospital

On reaching the incident scene the ambulance stops for 40 s of patient loading — during which its corridor is paused so cross streets are not held for a stationary vehicle — then it is automatically rerouted to the nearest hospital by Dijkstra travel time (not straight-line distance: one-way streets and current congestion decide). The signal corridor follows the new route immediately, and the whole second leg is logged on the same A-case. Amiri Hospital is inside the modelled area; Al-Sabah and Dar Al Shifa are represented by in-map anchor points on the corridors toward them.

7Speed-limit exemption — no fines

Emergency vehicles may exceed the posted limit

A dispatched ambulance with active lights runs at up to 150% of the posted limit (absolute cap 140 km/h), consistent with the emergency exemption in Kuwaiti traffic law. The enforcement camera at each junction is the same camera that detects the corridor: when it measures the ambulance above the limit with lights active, it logs "exemption applies — NO CITATION issued" as an operation instead of a fine. Lights off = no exemption: the ambulance drives as normal traffic. The Driver view shows the live speed beside the posted-limit sign with an EMERGENCY EXEMPT badge whenever the exemption is in effect.

Every exempt pass is on the operations record — auditable, per camera, per ambulance, per case.

8Demand-responsive signals for ordinary traffic

Early green for a lone driver — fair timers whenever anyone else is waiting

No ambulance involved: if exactly one approach of a junction is occupied and every other approach has been empty for 3 s, the junction moves through its own amber to the phase serving that approach — nobody sits at a red for an empty crossing (the late-night Mishref / Mubarak Al-Abdullah situation). The early green ends the moment any other approach becomes occupied (after a 5 s minimum green), when the lone traffic has passed, or at the 30 s cap — the junction then resumes its normal fixed-time cycle, so with several approaches occupied everyone gets the fair timer, no favourites. A 10 s per-junction cooldown prevents flip-flopping. Ambulance preemption always outranks this rule, and every grant and release is logged as an actuation operation; the map labels the junction "EARLY GREEN · lone traffic".

9Data provenance — what is real here

  • Real: the downtown Kuwait City road network, one-way system, turn restrictions and signal locations (OpenStreetMap), simulated at microscopic fidelity (SUMO 1.27, sublane model, rescue-lane behaviour).
  • Calibrated, not measured: background demand follows the published shape of Kuwaiti weekday traffic (07:00 morning peak by default; afternoon and evening peaks in the profile) — Kuwait exposes no public live traffic feed. When MOI/Municipality counts are available, drop them in data/real_counts.csv (hour,multiplier) and rebuild: they override the calibrated profile.
  • Synthetic: individual signal timing plans (static 72–90 s cycles generated per junction; the real KMoI plans can be substituted in the network file when obtained).

Scope of use

This is a planning, evaluation and training simulator. It is not a certified life-safety control system and must not be connected to live signal hardware without the full engineering, redundancy and regulatory review that real emergency-vehicle-preemption deployments require (conflict-monitor hardware, fail-to-normal guarantees, MOI approval). Decisions about real dispatch or real signal control must not be made on simulation output alone.
